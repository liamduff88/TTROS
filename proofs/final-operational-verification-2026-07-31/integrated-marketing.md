Public buying signals are useful.

They are not permission to blast someone with a half-personalized message 14 seconds later.

If a company posts a hiring signal, launches a new offer, changes leadership, opens a new location, or mentions a workflow problem publicly, that gives you context.

It does not remove the need for judgment.

The strongest sales ops systems I trust keep a human review gate in the loop:

Is this actually relevant?
Is there a clear reason to reach out?
Can we say something specific without pretending we know more than we do?
Would this message preserve trust if the buyer showed it to their team?

That is the difference between useful prospecting and automation theater.

For most growing service businesses, the next revenue lift is not “more automation.”

It is finding the strongest reviewable opportunity, deciding whether it deserves a send, and making the first message commercially grounded enough to start a real conversation.

Speed matters.

So does restraint.
